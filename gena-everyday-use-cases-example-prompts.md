# GenAI in Everyday Life — Prompt Examples

## 1. Learn & Understand

### Example 1: Learn a Concept

Teach me [topic] as a complete beginner. Use simple language, one everyday analogy, and two examples. Then ask me three questions, one at a time. Wait for my answer, give feedback, and do not move on until I understand.

### Example 2: Compound Interest

Explain compound interest to a 15-year-old using €100 growing over three years. Show the calculation year by year, compare it with simple interest, and finish with one short quiz question.

## 2. Write & Communicate

### Example 1: Rewrite a Message

Rewrite the message below so it sounds professional, warm, and concise. Keep the main meaning, use plain English, and do not invent facts. Give me two versions: a friendly version and a formal version.

**Message:** [Paste your draft]

### Example 2: Write a Professional Email

Write a professional email to my team reminding them to submit a report by Friday. Politely mention that receiving it earlier would be helpful. Keep the email concise, warm, and professional.

**Example output:**

**Subject:** Report Deadline

Hi team,

Please send the report by Friday. If you’re able to share it earlier, that would be very helpful.

Thank you!

## 3. Plan & Organize

### Example 1: Create a Plan

Help me plan [goal] over [time period]. I have [hours/budget/resources]. My top priorities are [priorities]. Create a realistic plan with tasks, estimated time, milestones, and a backup option. Ask questions if you need key details.

### Example 2: Learn Python

Help me plan learning basic Python over four weeks. I have 30 minutes on weekdays, my deadline is four weeks, and I prefer learning by building projects. Create week-by-week milestones, a 30-minute session structure, one mini-project per week, a progress checklist, and a catch-up plan if I miss a day.

## 4. Create & Brainstorm

### Example 1: Generate Video Ideas

Give me 8 creative ideas for a 60-second video teaching [topic] to [audience]. For each idea, include a hook, a simple story, the key learning point, and a closing question. Avoid clichés and keep the ideas distinct.

### Example 2: Develop a Video Outline

Develop idea 2 into a 6-scene outline. Keep each scene focused on one message and include narration plus suggested visuals.

## 5. Work with Information

### Example 1: Summarize and Extract Information

Use only the text I provide. Summarize it in 5 bullet points, then create a table with decisions, action items, owners, and deadlines. Mark any missing information as "not specified" rather than guessing.

**Text:** [Paste your document or meeting notes]

### Example 2: Turn Meeting Notes into Action Items

Transform these meeting notes into a structured action list. Identify tasks, owners, deadlines, pending decisions, and the next meeting. If an owner or date is missing, mark it as "not specified" rather than inventing information.

**Meeting notes:**
- Website draft due Friday.
- Lina will review the copy.
- Budget still needs approval.
- Meet again next Tuesday.

## 6. Everyday Problem-Solving

### Example 1: Troubleshoot a Problem

Help me troubleshoot [problem]. Ask me one question at a time, starting with the simplest checks. After each answer, suggest the next safe step. Explain what each step tests, and tell me when I should stop and contact a qualified professional.

### Example 2: Prepare for a Decision

Help me compare [option A] and [option B]. Ask me about my priorities and constraints. Create a comparison table showing advantages, disadvantages, costs, risks, and uncertainties. Do not make assumptions about my preferences; explain the trade-offs so I can decide.
